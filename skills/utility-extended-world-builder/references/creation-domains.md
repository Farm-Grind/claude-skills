## 1. DOMAIN FRAMEWORK OVERVIEW

Do not work through every domain in sequence for every task. Apply the depth gate:

- **Minimum viable**: physical, ecology, one exceptional system, daily life texture. Use for
  early-stage projects, short fiction, or when scope is deliberately narrow.
- **Full worldbuild**: all domains. Use for novel series, transmedia projects, games with
  persistent worlds, or projects requiring long-term lore consistency.
- **Deepen mode**: load only the section relevant to the requested element.

Always run the System Integration Cascade (Section 7) after any exceptional system is defined.

## 2. PHYSICAL & COSMOLOGICAL

- What type of world? (planet, station, plane, pocket dimension, dying sun, other)
- Size, gravity, atmosphere — how do these affect creature biology and movement?
- Celestial bodies: sun(s), moon(s), stars, their cycles, and how inhabitants mark time by them
- Climate zones and their distribution — what drives weather here?
- What is the cosmological model the inhabitants believe vs. what is actually true?
- Does the world have an origin story that matters to current inhabitants?
- What physical laws differ from baseline reality, and what are their downstream consequences?

---

## 3. ECOLOGY & RESOURCES

Herbert's principle: choose one physical constraint and trace it through everything.

- What is the most scarce resource, and what does scarcity do to the culture?
- What is the most abundant resource, and does abundance produce complacency or conflict?
- What is the dominant ecosystem? What species define it?
- What creatures exist that have no equivalent on Earth? What ecological niche do they fill?
- How does the ecology shape where people can live, what they can eat, how they travel?
- What has been extracted or destroyed, and what are the long-term consequences?
- What natural disasters occur? How frequently? How has culture adapted to survive them?

---

## 4. HISTORY & MYTHOLOGY

The world must contain more history than the story uses. Gaps imply depth.

- What happened before the story's present that everyone knows but no one fully understands?
- What ancient civilization left ruins, artifacts, or consequences still felt today?
- What was the world's greatest catastrophe? Its greatest achievement?
- What historical lie is accepted as truth? What truth is suppressed?
- What conflict shaped current borders, alliances, or social structures?
- What myths explain the world's origin, the gods, the exceptional systems?
- What do different cultures believe about the same historical event?

Tolkien's test: if you removed your story from this history, would the history still make
sense as a record of real events?

---

## 5. PEOPLE, RACES & SPECIES

Holism rule: cultures are systems. Change one element; trace what else changes.

- Who inhabits this world? In what proportions and in what distribution?
- What are the real differences between groups (biological, cultural, historical) vs. the
  perceived differences that drive prejudice and conflict?
- What does the dominant culture take for granted that a visitor would find extraordinary?
- What does the marginalized culture understand about the world that the dominant culture
  cannot see from its position?
- What family structures exist? (Not all worlds default to Western nuclear family.)
- How does kinship determine rights, obligations, and identity?
- What is the lived experience of an ordinary person in each major group?
- Le Guin's test: who in this world is invisible in most stories set here, and what does the
  world look like from their perspective?

---

## 6. MAGIC / TECHNOLOGY / EXCEPTIONAL SYSTEMS

Define the hard/soft stance before anything else. See Section 14.

For any exceptional system:

- What can it do? (capabilities)
- What can it NOT do? (limitations — more important than capabilities)
- What does it cost? (physical, psychological, social, moral)
- Who has access? (and what does that distribution do to power structures)
- How is it learned or acquired?
- What happens when it goes wrong?
- Is it understood or mysterious — and to whom?

After defining the system, run Section 7 immediately.

## 7. SYSTEM INTEGRATION CASCADE

Run this after every exceptional system (magic, technology, alien biology, physics alteration)
is defined. For each item below, state the consequence or mark OPEN if unresolved.

| Domain | Cascade question |
|---|---|
| Economy | How does this system change how wealth is created, held, or transferred? |
| Warfare | How does this system change how conflicts are won or lost? |
| Food & agriculture | How does this system affect how people grow, store, or distribute food? |
| Transportation | How does this system change how people and goods move? |
| Communication | How does this system change how information travels? |
| Social hierarchy | Who gains power from this system, and who loses it? |
| Religion | Is this system integrated into religious practice, in conflict with it, or is it religion? |
| Daily life | What does an ordinary person's day look like because of this system? |
| Gender & family | Does this system alter who bears children, raises them, or inherits? |
| Law & governance | Is this system regulated? Who enforces the rules? What are the penalties? |

A system that doesn't affect at least 5 of these domains is probably not load-bearing.
A system that affects all 10 is your world's spine. Build from there.

## 8. ECONOMY & DAILY LIFE

Malinda Lo's Five Foundations: Power, Rules, Rituals, Place, Food. These are interlocked.

**Food** is the fastest path to texture. What do common people eat? Where does food come from?
What is expensive? What is forbidden? What is eaten at celebrations, at funerals? What food
signals class, region, faction, or faith?

**Daily life audit for an ordinary person:**
- How do they wake up? What is the first thing they do?
- How do they get water? How do they get food?
- How do they travel? How far in a day?
- What do they do for work, and how is that work valued?
- What do they do for rest, entertainment, leisure?
- What do they fear on an ordinary day (not a plot day)?
- What do they hope for?

This person's ordinary day is where world physics become real. If the magic doesn't affect
this person's breakfast, it may not be load-bearing enough.

**Economy:**
- What is the medium of exchange, and who controls it?
- What is trade like within a community vs. between communities?
- What is the black market? What is illegal but common?
- What does poverty look like here? What does wealth look like?

## 9. CULTURE, RELIGION & RITUAL

- What rituals mark the major transitions of life (birth, adulthood, partnership, death)?
- What is considered sacred and what is considered profane?
- How does religion interact with political power — unified, competing, or subordinate?
- What do different classes or regions believe about the same religious questions?
- What art forms exist? What do they celebrate or mourn?
- What is the relationship between the dominant culture and its neighbors — imitation,
  competition, contempt, or curiosity?
- What taboos exist, and what is the consequence of breaking them?
- What is entertainment? What do people do when they are not working?

---

## 10. LANGUAGE & COMMUNICATION

Language does not need to be invented. But it must be thought about.

- How many languages or dialects exist? What does linguistic diversity signal about history?
- What does the dominant language's structure reveal about its culture's worldview?
  (Le Guin: language is the culture's self-model made audible)
- What words exist in this world that have no equivalent elsewhere? What concepts do they encode?
- What slang, profanity, or euphemism reveals social structure?
- How does literacy distribute? Who can read? What does literacy enable?
- How does information travel across distance? How fast? How reliably?

---

## 11. POLITICS & CONFLICT

- What is the political structure, and what historical process produced it?
- Where is power nominally held vs. where is power actually held?
- What is the current tension — the fault line that could produce the story's conflict?
- What groups are in competition, and what specifically do they compete for?
- What prevents the current system from collapsing, and how close is it to the edge?
- What does legitimate resistance look like? What does it look like from the other side?

## 12. LOCATION DEEPENING: SENSORY AUDIT

For every significant location, complete this before writing it. Architecture research finding:
worlds described only visually miss 30% of sensory experience that builds presence.

| Sense | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Sound | What is the ambient soundscape? What signals danger, safety, activity? |
| Smell | What does this place smell like on an ordinary day? After rain? After violence? |
| Touch | What surfaces do characters contact? Temperature? Texture? Weight of air? |
| Taste | What enters the mouth here — dust, salt, smoke, clean air? |
| Proprioception | How does the body feel moving here — easy, labored, constrained, free? |
| Emotional register | What does this place do to people's moods over time? |

Then: what does this place sound like in the rain? What does it smell like when it's burning?
What does it feel like to arrive for the first time vs. to have grown up here?

## 13. LOCATION DEEPENING: LIVED-IN CHECKLIST

A location that looks inhabited feels like a stage. A location that has been inhabited feels
like a place. Before finalizing any location:

- What evidence of repeated use is visible? (worn paths, polished surfaces, stained walls)
- What evidence of time passing is visible? (growth, decay, repair, accumulation)
- What evidence of past conflict is visible? (scars, blocked doors, rebuilt sections)
- What evidence of impermanence is visible? (temporary structures, abandoned projects)
- What do the objects here tell you about the people who use them — without explanation?
- What would a person who grew up here walk past without noticing?
- What would a stranger notice immediately that a native would never mention?

Environmental storytelling principle: every object in a location has a history. The history
does not need to be told. It needs to be implied.

## 14. HARD/SOFT SPECTRUM DECLARATION

For every exceptional system, declare its stance before proceeding.

**Hard system** (Sanderson model): rules are known, consistent, and can solve plot problems.
Limitations are more interesting than capabilities. If the reader understands the system,
it can serve as a plot engine.

**Soft system** (Le Guin / Tolkien model): rules exist but are partially or fully hidden.
Mystery is a feature. The system cannot be used to solve problems cleanly — but it creates
atmosphere, dread, wonder.

**Mixed**: hard in some domains (what it can do physically), soft in others (why it exists,
what it costs spiritually).

Sanderson's laws apply to any hard or mixed system:
1. Ability to solve conflicts is proportional to reader understanding of the system.
2. Limitations are more interesting than capabilities.
3. Expand what exists before adding something new. Deep beats wide.

State which stance this system takes, and be consistent.
