# Boxing Trainer

At-home boxing technique and conditioning for a fitness-focused practitioner
not pursuing sparring or competition. Goal: strong footwork, defensive head
movement, boxing-specific endurance, and correct technical foundation.
No partner needed. All drills executable in a basement with minimal equipment.

---

## PART 1 — FOUNDATION SEQUENCE

Build in this order. Do not skip levels. Each level is a prerequisite.

**Level 1 — Stance and stillness**
Before any movement, establish a rock-solid stance. Poor stability means
energy wasted on every drill and bad habits baked into footwork.

Orthodox (right-handed): left foot forward, feet slightly wider than
shoulder-width, weight balanced 55/45 front/back, knees soft, guard up
(hands at cheekbones, elbows tucked). Chin slightly down.

Drill: Stand in stance. Hold 60 seconds. Any wobble or weight shift is a
failure. Fix it before moving.

**Level 2 — Step-drag footwork**
The fundamental boxing movement pattern. 2–3 inch steps only — not strides.

Mechanics: Step with the foot on the side you're moving toward. Drag the
other foot to restore stance. Never let feet cross. Push off with toes on
the dragging foot. Reach with toes on the stepping foot.

Directions: forward, backward, left lateral, right lateral. Drill each
direction independently before combining. 3×2-minute rounds.

**Level 3 — Pivot**
Keep front foot planted. Use rear foot to change facing direction. Circle
around the fixed front foot through 360°, restoring full stance each step.

**Level 4 — Shadowboxing (movement only)**
Move in all directions using step-drag. No punches yet. Focus on smooth
weight transfer and maintaining stance throughout. 3-minute rounds.

**Level 5 — Shadowboxing (with punches)**
Add the four basic punches: jab (1), cross (2), hook (3), uppercut (4).
Integrate footwork — every combination includes a movement component.

## PART 2 — FOOTWORK DRILLS

Run these as dedicated drill rounds (2–3 min each), not mixed into
shadowboxing:

**Lateral shuffle:** Side-to-side step-drag, maintaining stance. Stay
low. 5×30 seconds.

**Box step:** Forward 6 steps → right 6 steps → back 6 steps → left 6
steps. Repeat and reverse direction. Keeps stance angles honest.

**Circle drill:** Place an object on the floor. Circle it using step-drag,
lead leg always pointing toward the object. Clockwise then counterclockwise.

**Pivot drill:** Front foot fixed. Pivot rear foot to face 12 new positions
(like a clock face). Full stance restored at each position.

**Agility ladder (or tape grid):** Mark a ladder pattern with tape on the
floor (6–8 rungs, 18" wide). Step patterns: in-out lateral, single-foot
hops, crossover steps. 3 passes per pattern.

## PART 3 — HEAD MOVEMENT

Four defensive movements. Build in isolation before integrating with
shadowboxing.

**Duck:** Drop straight down so punch clears over your head. No stance
adjustment. Drive from legs, not waist.

**Slip right (orthodox):** Move head right of an incoming jab. Pivot front
leg so knee/foot point right. Left shoulder fills space your head vacated.
Hands stay up.

**Slip left:** Move head left. Pivot rear leg, knee/foot point left. Right
shoulder fills vacated space. Critical: bend from legs, not waist.

**Pull back:** Without moving feet, chin down, shift weight to rear leg,
move head off the punch line. Small movement — 3–4 inches is enough.

**Key principle:** Head movement is not defense — it is repositioning to a
new attack angle. After every slip or duck, a counter punch follows. Train
the counter from day one.

### Head movement drills (solo)

**Slip line:** Tie a cord/hand wrap at head height across your training
space. Move along it, slipping left and right without touching it. Keep
movements small. Add counters after slips when comfortable. 3×2-minute
rounds.

**DIY maize ball:** Tie a tennis ball to a hand wrap, suspend at head
height. Swing it and practice slipping both sides. Builds reflex and
calibrates movement size.

**Shadowboxing with head movement:** After every combination, execute one
head movement. Pattern: throw 1-2 → slip right. Throw 1-2-3 → duck.
Vary the pairing.

**Tape line drill:** Mark a straight line on the floor with tape (10+ feet).
Stand with one foot on each side. Walk the line executing slips: slip left,
step forward, slip right, step forward, repeat. Move backward for return.

For full slip mechanics and combination integration:
See [references/head-movement.md](references/head-movement.md)

## PART 4 — SHADOWBOXING

The highest-leverage solo drill. Covers footwork, technique, conditioning,
and head movement simultaneously. Mirror recommended but not required.

### Session structure

Always shadowbox in rounds. Round = 3 minutes work, 1 minute rest.
Use a boxing timer app.

**Beginner:** 3 rounds
**Intermediate:** 5–6 rounds
**Conditioning focus:** 6–8 rounds

### Round focus progression

Build across a training block. In early sessions, each round has one focus:

| Round | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Movement only — no punches. Warm up footwork. |
| 2 | Jab only — step into it, retract, move. |
| 3 | 1-2 combinations with lateral movement after each. |
| 4 | 1-2-3 with pivot after hook. |
| 5 | Full combinations + head movement integration. |
| 6 | Open round — no scripted pattern. React to imagined opponent. |

As skill develops, compress this into fewer rounds and let all elements
combine simultaneously from round 1.

### Weighted shadowboxing

Hold 1–3 lb dumbbells during rounds 1–4. Drop weights for rounds 5–6.
Unweighted movement will feel faster and snappier. Do not use weights
heavier than 3 lbs — technique degradation outweighs conditioning benefit.

## PART 5 — CONDITIONING STRUCTURE

Boxing conditioning is anaerobic-dominant. Steady-state cardio (exercise
bike) builds base but does not substitute for boxing-specific interval work.

### Round-based HIIT (primary conditioning tool)

**Structure:** Work period = 3 minutes. Rest = 1 minute. Repeat 4–8 rounds.

Intensity within rounds: First 90 seconds moderate pace, last 90 seconds
push output. Do not sandbag the final minute.

**Jump rope:** Primary auxiliary conditioning tool. Builds footwork rhythm,
cardiovascular fitness, and ankle/calf conditioning simultaneously.
- Beginner: 3×2-minute rounds with 1-minute rest
- Intermediate: 5×3-minute rounds with 1-minute rest
- Advanced: 10-minute continuous, varied patterns (alternate feet, high
  knees, double unders)

No rope available: simulate rope motion with hands and bounce on balls of
feet. Less effective but maintains the movement pattern.

### Sample boxing conditioning session

| Block | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Jump rope / jog in place | 5 min |
| Footwork drills | Step-drag directions, box step, pivot | 2×2 min |
| Shadowboxing | 4–6 rounds, progressive focus | 16–24 min |
| Head movement | Slip line or maize ball | 2×2 min |
| Bodyweight circuit | Push-ups, squats, planks, burpees | 3 rounds |
| Cool-down | Static stretch: hips, shoulders, calves | 5 min |

Total: 40–50 minutes.

### Bodyweight circuit for boxing (3 rounds, 45s work / 15s rest)

Push-ups → Squat jumps → Plank → Lateral shuffles → Mountain climbers →
Burpees → Side plank (each side)

## PART 6 — EQUIPMENT PROGRESSION

### Tier 1 — Buy immediately, zero delay

**Jump rope** (~$15–30): Highest ROI piece of equipment. Buy first.
Crossrope or any weighted speed rope. Do not buy a cheap light rope.

**Boxing timer app** (free): Structure all sessions in rounds from day one.
Search "boxing interval timer" — many free options.

**Hand wraps** (~$10): Required before any bag work. 180" wraps, cotton or
elastic blend.

### Tier 2 — Buy when shadowboxing plateaus (4–8 weeks in)

**Heavy bag, 70–100 lbs** (~$100–200): Hanging bag preferred for basement
with ceiling joists — more realistic swing than freestanding. Provides
resistance, teaches distance, and trains real punch mechanics.

**Boxing gloves, 12–16 oz** (~$40–80): Required with bag. Do not hit bag
without gloves and wraps. 14 oz recommended for training.

### Tier 3 — Add when fundamentals are solid

**Agility ladder** (~$20): Or tape markings on the floor (free equivalent).
Footwork pattern drills.

**Double-end bag** (~$40–60): Ceiling and floor anchored. Trains reflexes
and accuracy better than maize ball once punching fundamentals exist.

**Floor mirror** (optional, high value): Real-time stance and head movement
feedback. Partial substitute for a coach's eye.

### DIY tools (zero cost)

**Slip line:** Twine or paracord tied at head height between two fixed
points. More effective than many commercial tools for solo head movement.

**DIY maize ball:** Tennis ball + hand wrap + ceiling anchor. Swing and
slip. Covers the same function as commercial maize ball.

**Tape agility ladder:** Tape on basement floor. Identical training value
to a commercial ladder.

## PART 7 — INTEGRATION WITH STRENGTH TRAINING

**Scheduling rules (non-negotiable):**
- Never heavy strength on the same day as high-intensity boxing
- Skill days (technique focus) pair acceptably with accessory lifting
- When same-day training is unavoidable: boxing first, lifting second
- Allow 48 hours between heavy compound lifting and hard boxing conditioning

**Strength work that directly benefits boxing:**
- Trap bar deadlifts → posterior chain power transfer to punches
- Pull-ups → lat strength for punch retraction speed and guard endurance
- Bulgarian split squats → single-leg stability for footwork
- Pallof press → anti-rotational core for combination punching
- Jump squats → rate of force development for push-off

**What to avoid:**
- Heavy bench press volume if shoulder mobility is limited — use dumbbell
  alternatives
- Heavy direct bicep/tricep work before boxing sessions — creates fatigue
  in punching muscles
- Any lift that leaves CNS depleted on boxing skill days

## PART 8 — FLEXIBLE SESSION FORMATS (ADHD AND BUSY SCHEDULES)

Boxing sessions do not require 45 minutes to be effective. Match the format
to available time. Decide at session start — no planning required mid-session.

### Session tiers

| Time available | Format | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 15 min | Micro | Jump rope 3 min warm-up → 2 focused rounds (one drill each) |
| 20–25 min | Short | Jump rope → 3–4 shadowboxing rounds with one focus per round |
| 35–45 min | Standard | Full session: footwork drills → shadowboxing → head movement |
| 50–60 min | Extended | Standard + bodyweight circuit or slip line deep work |

**Micro session example:** 3 minutes jump rope → Round 1: step-drag only,
all directions → Round 2: shadowboxing with jab-cross and lateral movement.
Done. This is a complete session. It maintains technique and habit.

### Prescribing by focus, not by duration

When asked what to work on without a time constraint, rotate focus:

**Session A (footwork):** Step-drag drills → box step → pivot drill →
shadowboxing with movement emphasis

**Session B (head movement):** Slip line → DIY maize ball → shadowboxing
with slip-then-counter after every combination

**Session C (conditioning):** Jump rope HIIT → full shadowboxing rounds →
bodyweight circuit

Cycling through A → B → C provides novelty across the week, trains all three
skill areas, and prevents the staleness that drives ADHD dropout.

### Low-motivation sessions

On days where starting feels hard: prescribe Session A micro tier. Footwork
only, 15 minutes. The drill is mechanical, requires no emotional engagement,
and is the technical foundation everything else rests on. It always counts.

## EXAMPLES

**"What should I work on today?"**
→ Ask what block they're in (A/B/C) and last session type. Prescribe
opposite focus: if last session was technique, prescribe conditioning.
Always start with footwork warm-up regardless.

**"How do I get better at slipping?"**
→ Prescribe slip line drill (3×2-minute rounds). Add DIY maize ball if
available. Integrate slip-then-counter into shadowboxing. Check they're
bending from legs not waist.

**"My footwork feels clunky."**
→ Return to Level 1 and 2. Step-drag drill in isolation, 3×2-minute rounds.
Check step size (should be 2–3 inches, not strides). Check stance width
not collapsing during movement.

**"Should I get a heavy bag?"**
→ Only if shadowboxing feels limited — typically after 4–8 consistent weeks.
If yes, prescribe Tier 2 equipment in sequence: wraps → gloves → bag.

**"How do I structure my boxing rounds?"**
→ Apply sample conditioning session above. Confirm current round capacity.
Prescribe appropriate round count (3 beginner, 5–6 intermediate, 6–8
conditioning focus).

**"I only have 15 minutes."**
→ Prescribe micro tier: 3 min jump rope → 2 focused rounds. Name the focus
for each round. Do not suggest skipping. 15 minutes of boxing is 15 minutes
of boxing.

**"I haven't trained in two weeks."**
→ Return with Session A micro tier. Footwork only. No pressure, no catch-up.
The next session after that: Session A short. Rebuild from the floor.
