## Mechanics

### Movement categories

**Angular head movement (slips, ducks):** Short, sharp, directional.
Faster and more energy efficient. Use as primary defensive tool.

**Circular head movement (rolls, weaves):** Longer, flowing arcs.
More energy-intensive but harder to time a counter against. Use to
follow hooks and during pressure situations.

**Pull back:** Linear. Shifts weight to rear leg, no stepping. Best
against straight punches when footwork is not yet an option.

### Critical technique cues

- Bend from the legs, never the waist. Waist-bending leaves you off
  balance and slower to recover.
- Small movements — punches should miss by inches, not feet. Larger
  movements are slower and expose you to follow-up punches longer.
- Keep hands up during every head movement. As the head slips left,
  the guard shifts to fill the vacated space on the right.
- Head movement leads to counter position — know what punch follows each
  slip before the movement happens.

### Head position awareness (slots)

After each movement your head lands in a new position (slot). Different
punches are available from each slot:

- Slip right → right side of opponent's center → counter with left hook
  to body, left hook to head, or right uppercut
- Slip left → left side → counter with right cross or right uppercut
- Duck → low → counter with both hooks to body
- Pull back → distance created → counter with jab or 1-2 as they
  follow forward

Train the counter from day one. Defense without counter teaches passive
boxing and removes half the purpose of head movement.

## Combination Integration

### Basic slip patterns

**After jab (1):** Slip right → counter with left hook (3)
**After cross (2):** Slip left → counter with right cross (2) or
  right uppercut (4)
**After 1-2:** Slip right → counter with 3 or slip left → counter with 2

### Tape line progression

Set up a 10–12 foot tape line on the floor. Stand with one foot each side.

**Forward pass:**
- Step forward → slip away from imaginary jab (rear leg pivot)
- Step forward → cross → slip rear leg (slipping the counter jab)
- Step forward → lead hook → pivot out on lead leg
- Step with final jab → shift weight to front foot

**Backward pass:**
- Step forward behind jab → slip to rear leg (inside slip)
- Step forward with cross → slip toward lead leg
- Lead hook → shift weight back to rear leg
- Continue to end of line

Walk this drill slowly at first. The goal is muscle memory for combining
movement and offense — not speed.

### Shadowboxing integration pattern

Beginner pattern (add after round 3 competence):
1. Throw 1-2
2. Immediately slip right
3. Throw 3 (left hook) from the slip position
4. Step back with step-drag

Intermediate pattern:
1. Jab → cross → slip right → left hook to body
2. Jab → slip left → cross → pivot out
3. Double jab → cross → roll under imaginary hook → step out

Drill each pattern until it flows before adding the next.

## Solo Drill Progressions

### Slip line (progressive)

Week 1–2: Stationary. Stand at slip line. Slip left and right alternately.
Focus on leg bend, small movement, hands staying up.

Week 3–4: Add forward movement. Move along the line slipping as you advance.
This simulates moving into pressure.

Week 5+: Add counters. Slip left → throw right cross. Slip right → throw
left hook. The slip and counter become one fluid motion.

Advanced: Backward movement slips. Slip as you retreat — simulates slipping
while giving ground against pressure.

### Maize ball (progressive)

Hang tennis ball at head height. Swing it directly toward your face.

Stage 1: Single slip. Swing → slip one side. Ball swings back → reset.
Stage 2: Alternating slips. Swing → slip right → ball swings back → slip
left as it returns.
Stage 3: Slip + counter. Slip → throw one punch into the space the ball
vacated → return to guard before ball returns.
Stage 4: Continuous rhythm. Keep the ball swinging, slip each pass, add
counters.

Do not try to hit the ball initially — focus on movement quality. Hitting
comes after slipping is automatic.

## Common Errors

**Bending from the waist:** Creates imbalance, slows recovery, telegraphs
the move. Fix: think of moving hips, not bending back.

**Crossing the centerline with chin:** During slip right, chin must not
cross over the knee. This is the safe range — past it you are exposed.

**Dropping hands during movement:** Guard opens as head moves. Counter:
practice consciously keeping hands at cheekbones through every slip.

**Over-slipping:** Moving 12 inches to avoid a punch that needs 3 inches.
Wastes time and energy. Calibrate with maize ball — smallest move that
clears the ball.

**Not countering:** Training head movement without a counter is training
passivity. Every slip leads to a punch. Build this as reflex from the start.

**Rhythm-only slipping:** Slipping to a predictable beat is easy to time.
Vary the timing. Slip fast, slow, early, late. Unpredictability is the goal.
