## Core Principle

Progressive overload means the body receives a training stimulus that exceeds
its current capacity, forcing adaptation. Without it, training becomes
maintenance. The body adapts to a given stimulus within 4–6 weeks and stops
changing if the stimulus doesn't change.

Key distinction: progressive overload is not about always doing MORE — it is
about whether the current stimulus is still challenging. If you can complete
all reps and sets with excellent form and moderate effort, overload is no
longer occurring.

## Seven Overload Methods

Apply in this order when a method plateaus:

**1. Add reps (use first)**
Easiest progression. If your target is 3×8 and you hit 3×8 cleanly, try 3×9
next session. When you hit the top of your rep range on all sets, move to
method 2.

**2. Add load**
Upper body compounds: 2.5–5 lb increments.
Lower body compounds: 5–10 lb increments.
Isolation exercises: 2.5 lb only. A 5 lb jump on a lateral raise is 25%
— too large.

**3. Add sets**
When both reps and load options are plateaued, add one set to the exercise.
Do not exceed 20 sets per muscle group per week.

**4. Reduce rest periods**
Use only on accessory/isolation work. Do not reduce rest on heavy compound
lifts — quality of motor unit recruitment drops and injury risk rises.
Reduce by 10–15 seconds per progression point.

**5. Increase range of motion**
Squatting deeper, lowering barbell further on bench, fuller ROM on pull-ups.
ROM increases produce greater muscle tension and hypertrophy stimulus even
at the same load. Improve mobility first; don't force depth.

**6. Improve tempo**
Slow the eccentric (lowering) phase to 2–4 seconds. Increases time under
tension and muscle fiber recruitment without changing load.

**7. Exercise progression**
Moving from easier to harder variation of a movement (goblet squat → front
squat → back squat). Reserve for when load-based progression has extended
limits.

## Progression Schemes by Goal

### Strength focus (3–6 reps)
- Progress load every session if form is clean
- Stop set before failure — skill acquisition requires controlled reps
- Rest 3–5 minutes between working sets
- When progress stalls: reduce reps, add load, reset

### Hypertrophy focus (8–12 reps)
- Double progression: add reps until top of range hit on all sets, then
  add load and reset to bottom of range
- Example: 3×8 → 3×9 → 3×10 → 3×11 → 3×12 → add 5 lbs → 3×8
- Rest 2–3 minutes between sets
- Train 1–2 reps short of failure

### Power/explosiveness (3–5 reps, intent to move fast)
- Load stays 60–80% of 1RM
- Progression is bar speed, not load
- Progress by adding 5 lbs when reps feel sluggish — not too fast
- Rest 3–4 minutes (neural quality degrades with insufficient rest)

### Endurance (15+ reps, circuits)
- Progress by adding reps, then reducing rest between sets
- Load progression is last — endurance is about density and volume

## Common Mistakes

**Adding weight before mastering form.** Stalls progress and causes joint
stress. Form failure is a ceiling, not a challenge.

**Progressing every exercise simultaneously.** Pick 2–3 primary lifts to
actively overload. Let accessories follow without forcing progression.

**Skipping deload.** Body adapts and then needs consolidation. Without
planned deloads every 4 weeks, fatigue accumulates until injury forces rest.

**Ignoring daily readiness.** Sleep-deprived, highly stressed, or sick days
are not progression days. Maintain load, reduce volume.

**Adding 10 lbs to a 20 lb dumbbell.** Context matters. Small absolute
jumps on small loads are large percentage increases. Bodyweight and small
load exercises require fractional plates or rep-only progression.

## Tracking

Track every session. Without data, overload is guesswork.

Minimum required data: exercise, sets, reps, load.

Pattern to review weekly:
- Are primary lifts progressing across the block?
- Are any lifts stalled for 2+ sessions?
- Is session RPE increasing at the same loads? (indicates fatigue, not plateau)

A stalled lift at stable RPE = true plateau → apply next overload method.
A stalled lift at rising RPE = fatigue → deload before progressing.
